Why it happens

Why your gut reacts to how you feel

The nerves

Your gut has its own nervous system. Around 500 million nerve cells line your digestive tract, in constant conversation with your brain through the vagus nerve.

Under stress, your body shifts its resources to fight or flight, and digestion slows, speeds up, tightens or stops. That's why nerves hit the stomach, why grief takes away your appetite, and why a bad day can bloat you even on a good diet.

The 2,000-year-old version

Chinese medicine described the same thing in a different language. Each organ system has an emotion that affects it most.

  • Spleen and Stomach · Earth

    They turn food into energy. Their emotion is worry. Overthinking tires them, and food sits instead of moving.

  • Liver · Wood

    It keeps things flowing. Its emotion is frustration. When it's blocked, it pushes sideways into the gut: knots, cramps, reflux, wind.

  • Lungs and Large Intestine · Metal

    They govern letting go. Their emotion is grief. When it's heavy, things get stuck.

  • Heart · Fire

    It holds the racing mind. When it's unsettled, it shows up as anxiety and dread in the belly.

Tapping works on both at once. You tap on acupuncture points with your fingertips while you name what you feel. The nervous system settles, and the gut follows.
